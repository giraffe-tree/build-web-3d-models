# Visual Review Ledger

The builder reviews used the running Web route at a locked 1440 × 900 CSS viewport, semantic state `rest`, fixed time `0`, and the named camera preset. A final integration review recaptured asset-only evidence at 1280 × 720, still above the manifest minimum. Original round images are retained beside this ledger.

## Round 1 — construction and first material pass

Evidence: `round-1-hero.png`, `round-1-orbit-a.png`, `round-1-orbit-b.png`.

Largest visible defects, ranked:

1. The rectangular suspension membrane visibly projected beyond the elliptical back frame at the top, bottom, and corners. This broke the most identity-critical silhouette and made the back read as an unfinished plane.
2. Key/environment energy and exposure pushed graphite, textile, and rubber toward the same near-white response. The chair lost its intended graphite/copper/basalt hierarchy.
3. The high-contrast alpha weave generated large moiré/radial interference patterns at the hero camera.

Highest-impact correction: reshaped every membrane row with the same rounded-cap envelope as the authored frame, removing all rectangular overhang.

Additional changes: reduced PMREM/environment, key, rim, fill, and tone-mapping exposure; lowered weave contrast and frequency.

Visible result: the back became one enclosed tensioned system; graphite, warm textile, rubber, and copper separated again. Residual weave moiré remained the next defect.

## Round 2 — final presentation pass

Evidence: `round-2-hero.png` plus the subsequently locked five final camera views.

Largest visible defects, ranked:

1. The alpha-tested back still showed concentric moiré, especially in the hero and front orbit views.
2. Initial fixed cameras overfilled the frame; one orbit clipped the back top and the close proof allowed portfolio copy to cover the mechanism.
3. A PMREM blur-sample warning appeared in the console even though the scene rendered correctly.

Highest-impact correction: replaced binary alpha cutouts with a mip-filtered, low-contrast woven color/bump response on a controlled translucent membrane. The copper yoke remains visible through the material without pixel interference.

Additional changes: re-authored camera distances around the 50–75% evidence occupancy range, gave Orbit A extra headroom, hid narrative copy only in the mechanism close view, clamped frame-time sampling, and reduced environment blur sigma to the supported range.

Visible result: no membrane overhang, no visible moiré, no clipped final view, no proof-view overlay, and clean console output in a fresh browser tab. Remaining limitations are the intentionally stylized oval back silhouette and the absence of animated adjustment endpoints.

## Round 3 — evidence purity and independent authority

The first locked non-hero images still carried portfolio UI. That made it impossible to tell whether a critic was scoring the asset or the page. Capture mode now removes the masthead, narrative copy, view rail, footer, diagnostics, and decorative overlay while fixing the named camera and disabling orbit input. Four review views were recaptured, inspected, hashed, and passed to a fresh critic with only the original request and the exact five final PNGs.

The critic found that the pixels still did not prove the back membrane's tension/load behavior, a credible seat-tilt/back-yoke/gas-lift mechanism, caster forks/axes, or actual orbit continuity. Its **73/100 — partial** decision overrides the builder's provisional complete claim.
